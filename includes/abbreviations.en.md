<!-- abbreviations -->
*[Standard plan]: Professional, Advanced, and Expert plans.
*[PLUS plan]: Professional PLUS, Advanced PLUS, and Expert PLUS plans.
*[PLUS plan and above]: Professional PLUS, Advanced PLUS, Expert PLUS, and Enterprise plans.
*[CYBER Coins]: Virtual credits in CYBERBIZ used to pay for payment, logistics, and selected system services. They exchange with New Taiwan dollars at a fixed 1:1 rate, and every charge has a fee record you can look up.
*[LIFF]: LINE Front-end Framework. A framework for running web apps inside the LINE app.
*[LINE OA]: LINE Official Account. An account a business or brand opens on LINE to talk with customers, send marketing messages, and provide customer service.
*[LAP]: LINE Ads Platform. LINE's performance advertising platform.
*[OG Image]: Open Graph Image. The image a platform such as Facebook, X, LinkedIn, LINE, or Slack pulls in when someone shares a page link.
*[CTR]: Click-through rate. The share of people who see your link in search results, ads, or social posts and actually click through to the site.
*[SERP]: Search engine results page. The list of results a person sees after searching on Google, Bing, or another search engine.
*[UID]: Unique identifier. A string or number assigned to one user, device, order, or file so the system can tell it apart from every other record.
*[2FA]: Two-factor authentication, shown in the admin as Two-Step Verification. After the account password, the user must also enter a second check, usually a code from a phone, so a stolen password alone cannot sign in.
*[image alt text]: A short description of an image, stored in the page code. It appears when the image cannot load, is read aloud by screen readers, and helps search engines understand the image.
*[GTM]: Google Tag Manager. A free tag manager that lets you add and update marketing tracking tags from one container snippet, without editing the site's source code each time.
*[ticket splitting]: When a customer buys several of the same e-ticket, the system issues one QR code (redemption code) that can be redeemed more than once. Ticket splitting breaks that code into separate tickets, each with its own QR code, so each ticket can be used or given away on its own.
*[driver pickup]: A carrier sends a driver to the address you set, usually your office or warehouse, to collect parcels for an order.
*[comment out code]: Wrapping code in comment marks so that part does not run. Use it to turn a feature off while testing, and keep the original code so you can restore it later.
*[product link]: The unique URL the system creates for a product page. Use it to promote the product, share it, or open that product from another system.
*[image hosting]: An external service that stores images and gives you a URL, so you can place the image on a site or in a file without uploading the file into CYBERBIZ.
*[product information]: Everything set on a product for the storefront, sales, and marketing, including basic settings, images, videos, and variants.
*[product name]: A required field when you create a product. It is the product's main title on the product page, order details, marketing campaigns, and reports.
*[product URL]: The path of a product page, used to open the page on the storefront and to let search engines index it.
*[product slogan]: A short line on the product page for a selling point or campaign message, such as an offer, a feature, or a brand claim.
*[product brief]: A short description on the product page that sums up what the product is and why it matters.
*[product page]: The storefront page for one product. It shows the name, price, images, options, stock, shipping methods, and the buy button.
*[product channel]: The setting that records where a product is fulfilled. When the same product can ship from different warehouses, stores, or vendors, the product channel keeps those sources separate.
*[fulfillment channel]: The warehouse, store, or vendor that actually ships the product.
*[shipping fee settings]: The carriers the store can use and the rules that set their shipping fees.
*[shipping temperature zone]: The temperature zone a product can ship in, such as room temperature, refrigerated, or frozen.
*[combo product]: A product sold as one item but made of two or more sub-products. It has its own combo price. Shipping, inventory, reports, and API data still follow the sub-products.
*[sub-product]: One product inside a combo. It keeps its own list price and product information, and it is sold only as part of the combo price.
*[pre-order]: A product that stays available after stock hits zero because "When out of stock" is set to keep selling. Customers can still place orders, and you do not need a separate product type.
*[specified combo]: A combo whose contents you fix in advance. Customers cannot swap the items inside it.
*[add-on price]: The price a customer pays to add an upsell product after buying a qualifying product.
*[combo product price difference]: The discount a sub-product receives inside a combo: its original price minus the final price after the combo discount is split across the sub-products.
*[optional combo]: A combo where you set which sub-products customers may choose, and customers pick the items and quantities.
*[total optional combo quantity]: How many sub-products a customer must pick in an optional combo. If you set it to 5, the customer must choose 5 sub-products before the combo can go into the cart.
*[related products]: A group of products you link by hand so they can be recommended together on the storefront.
*[Google product category]: The Google product category sent in the Google Merchant Center product feed. If you leave it blank, Google assigns a category.
*[warehouse integration]: Connecting the online store (EC) to the warehouse system (WMS) so orders, stock, and shipping status stay in sync.
*[variant]: A sellable unit made from option values, such as Red + Size M.
*[product option]: A label that distinguishes versions of a product, such as color, size, volume, or material.
*[option value]: One choice under a product option, such as Red or Blue under Color.
*[delivery conditions]: The combination of temperature zone, shipping method, and fulfillment channel that decides how a product ships, how the fee is calculated, and whether checkout splits the order.
*[delivery condition binding]: Setting a product so it can use, or can only use, specific logistics, a shipping temperature zone, or a fulfillment channel.
*[one-time credit card payment]: A credit card charge collected in full in one billing cycle, with no installments and no split charges.
*[outbound shipping]: The trip from you or the warehouse to the customer. This is the normal shipping flow.
*[reverse logistics]: The trip from the customer back to you or the warehouse, including returns, exchanges, and recalls.
*[return period]: The longest time a customer has to request a return, counted from when they receive the product. After it ends, the storefront request closes and you handle the return in the admin.
*[profit sharing]: Splitting revenue from a sale with a partner at a rate you set in advance, such as for a collaboration or a third-party sales channel.
*[3D Secure]: An extra identity check on an online card payment. At checkout the cardholder confirms the payment with a one-time password (OTP) or the bank's verification page, which reduces unauthorized charges.
*[OTP]: One-time password. A temporary password from the system or the bank for an online payment or identity check. It works once and only for a short time.
*[B2C]: Business to consumer. A business sells products or services directly to the end customer.
*[B2C bulk shipping]: You send a batch of parcels to the convenience store logistics center, by your own vehicle or a carrier, instead of handing them in at a store counter.
*[EC]: E-commerce. Selling through the internet, from showing products through checkout, payment, and shipping. In CYBERBIZ this is the online store.
*[COD]: Cash on delivery. The customer pays in cash when the parcel arrives, instead of paying online at checkout. Common on home delivery.
*[CPA]: Cost per action. Ad spend divided by the number of conversions, such as purchases, sign-ups, or clicks.
*[C2C]: Consumer to consumer. People buy and sell through a third-party platform rather than from a business.
*[EDM]: Electronic direct mail. A marketing email you send to a chosen audience, usually with HTML layout and performance tracking, rather than a one-to-one message.
*[UTM]: Urchin Tracking Module. Parameters added to the end of a URL so you can see which campaign, source, and path brought a visitor to the site.
*[WMS]: Warehouse Management System. The system that runs receiving, shipping, inventory, picking, and delivery. In CYBERBIZ this is Smart Warehousing.
*[POS]: Point of Sale. The in-store system for checkout, payment, and receipts, which also records sales, stock changes, and revenue. In CYBERBIZ this is Smart POS.
*[MBE]: Meta Business Extension. Meta's merchant tool for connecting the Facebook Conversions API, Pixel, product catalog, Instagram Shopping, and a Facebook Page in one setup.
*[FBE]: Facebook Business Extension, the former name of MBE.
*[GA]: Google Analytics. Google's tool for tracking how people use your site.
*[GA4]: Google Analytics 4, the current version of Google Analytics. It records interactions as events and supports cross-platform reporting.
*[GSC]: Google Search Console. Google's free tool for monitoring search performance, index status, and SEO issues, and for submitting a sitemap.
*[GMC]: Google Merchant Center. Google's product data platform. You store, check, and submit product data so products can appear in Google Shopping, search, YouTube, and Google Ads.
*[SEO]: Search engine optimization. On a store this covers product page titles, descriptions, URL structure, image alt text, and keywords, so pages rank higher in search.
*[SKU]: Stock keeping unit. The unique code for a product or variant, used for inventory, product data, and sales reports.
*[coupon]: A member-only offer the system assigns to a member account, such as a discount, free shipping, or a gift.
*[promo code]: A discount code a customer types in at checkout. It is not assigned to one member account, and it can apply store-wide as a discount, free shipping, or a gift.
*[ROAS]: Return on ad spend. Revenue from ads divided by ad spend. A higher number means the ads returned more revenue per dollar spent.
*[Expert plan and above]: Expert, Professional PLUS, Advanced PLUS, Expert PLUS, and Enterprise plans.
*[Advanced plan and above]: Advanced, Expert, Professional PLUS, Advanced PLUS, Expert PLUS, and Enterprise plans.
*[RWD]: Responsive web design. The site layout adjusts to desktop, tablet, and phone screens.
*[ERP]: Enterprise resource planning. A system that connects internal operations such as finance, inventory, purchasing, and production, and can sync orders, products, and stock with CYBERBIZ.
